## Top 3 Things That Matter Today

1. **Global bond selloff is the dominant macro regime — yields at multi-decade highs.** 10-yr at 4.784%, 30-yr at 5.26% and approaching the 5% psychological mark not seen in nearly two decades. UK gilts hit 1998 levels; Japan 10-yr briefly touched 3% for the first time in 30 years. A US-Iran military exchange drove Brent crude to $94.65/bbl (+4.6% Tuesday). Fed Chair Warsh is hawkish and markets are pricing ~70% probability of a rate hike this month — the highest it's been all year. Capital is rotating out of equities and bonds into precious metals and oil. This is a genuine macro regime shift, not a one-day wobble, and every high-multiple name on the watchlist has a valuation haircut applied until yields reverse.

2. **AVGO Q3 FY2026 results are out — stellar AI revenue, but Q4 guidance disappointed.** Revenue hit $29.6B (+86% YoY, a new record), AI semiconductor revenue was approximately $16B (+200%+ YoY), and EPS of $3.32 beat the $3.16 consensus. But Q4 revenue guidance of $34.8B came in below the Street's expectations, which initially sent the stock down ~4%. With AI chip revenue compounding this fast, the miss is in the guidance bar the market had set, not in the fundamental business — but in a rising-yield environment, the stock can't absorb disappointment the way it could at lower rates.

3. **NFP Friday is the week's fulcrum.** ADP employment data is due today; nonfarm payrolls arrive Friday. With 10-yr yields already at 4.784%, a hot jobs print would reinforce the rate-hike thesis and likely accelerate the bond selloff — bad for the entire growth/semi complex. A cool number could take some pressure off yields and allow a relief rally. Today's ADP read is the forward indicator; treat 8:30 AM ET as a decision point.

## Rotation Tracker

**Full de-risk, not a sector rotation.** This is not money moving from AI semis into defensives — it is capital leaving equities and bonds simultaneously, flowing into precious metals and oil. SOXX is down 2% alongside mega-cap tech (GOOGL, MSFT, AAPL), with no visible separation between semis and large-cap tech as relative safety plays. Both are high-multiple, long-duration assets that feel the rate compression the same way. Defensives (COST, WMT) may show relative strength but no data supports a meaningful bid there yet — this is a yield-driven repricing of everything that discounts future earnings, not a risk-on/risk-off sector rotation within equities.
