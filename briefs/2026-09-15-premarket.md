## Top 3 Things That Matter Today

1. **AI-slowdown essay detonates chip stocks.** Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a weekend essay calling for AI companies to slow development, citing fears that AI agents could "take over the entire internet" within 6–12 months. OpenAI's Sam Altman agreed publicly. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 5.1% on Monday, and the selling is bleeding into Tuesday's pre-market. This is the single biggest short-term driver across the entire watchlist.

2. **10-year Treasury breaks 5.04% — highest since 2007.** The yield crossed the critical 5% level overnight, driven by energy inflation (Brent crude at $107.30/bbl, +1.6%) and rising rate-hike expectations. The 30-year hit 5.4%. Rising real yields compress growth-stock multiples and add a second leg to the AI-name selloff.

3. **FOMC meeting in session; rate decision tomorrow (Sep 16).** Markets are pricing ~92% probability of a 25bps hike to 3.75–4.00%, which would be the first hike since July 2023. Chair Warsh's press conference and the updated dot plot will set the macro tone for Q4. Nasdaq futures are -0.68% and S&P 500 futures -0.58% ahead of the open.

## Sector Temperature: **RISK-OFF**

Three converging headwinds:
- **Sentiment shock**: The Amodei/Altman AI-slowdown alignment is the first time two of the most credible AI-lab CEOs have publicly pumped the brakes on AI acceleration. Even if the market intellectually understands this doesn't mean less chip demand immediately, the narrative is in the headlines.
- **Rates**: 10-year at 5%+ reprices growth stocks mechanically; the FOMC hike tomorrow (widely expected) will be a show-me moment for whether Warsh signals pause or continued tightening.
- **Energy**: Brent at $107 keeps inflation sticky and reduces the probability of a dovish surprise.

Until the FOMC press conference tomorrow, the path of least resistance is lower for AI names and semis. A dovish Warsh tone Wednesday could reverse some of this. For now: tight stops, watch MU into Sep 30, and VST for a potential mean-reversion setup given its 47% discount to consensus.
