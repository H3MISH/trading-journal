# EOD Market Brief — July 10, 2026

## Market Recap

The S&P 500 gained +0.8% to 7,543.64, the Nasdaq surged +1.3% to 26,206.89, and the Dow added +0.27% to 52,478.41, but breadth was strikingly narrow — nine of eleven S&P sectors closed red. All index gains were concentrated in Information Technology (+1.2%) and Energy (+1.8%), carried almost entirely by two company-specific catalysts: Meta's sweeping AI cloud/chip announcement (+6.85%) and Apple's confirmed $30B+ multi-year chip deal with Broadcom (+4.2%). SK Hynix's landmark Nasdaq debut (SKHY: priced $149, opened $170 — largest-ever US IPO by a foreign company) simultaneously repriced competitive risk for memory names, dragging MU and MRVL lower. VIX fell 6.3% to 15.84; volume of 14.7B shares was well below the 20-session average of 22.9B, confirming thin conviction behind the move.

## Rotation Tracker

**SOXX -0.5–1% | AVGO/META (AI platform) +4–7% >> AMD flat | MU/MRVL (memory/networking) -2–4% >> mega-cap FAANG flat/slight negative >> defensives lagging**

Today's rotation was surgical: capital moved specifically INTO AI application platforms (META +6.85% on cloud compute launch) and AI custom silicon (AVGO +4.2% on Apple deal) while rotating OUT of commodity memory (MU -2.0%) and high-multiple networking ASIC names (MRVL -4.1%). AMD, despite a positive Goldman note, couldn't sustain Thursday's move — the market is differentiating between "near-term AI revenue visibility today" (META, AVGO) and "AI demand adjacency with execution risk" (AMD, MRVL). Defensives (COST, WMT) were left entirely behind; NFLX's -2.78% drop confirms even secular growth names face pre-earnings pressure when the AI megacap narrative dominates the tape. The SK Hynix IPO added a structural nuance: the market is pricing in intensified HBM competition rather than pure demand upside for the memory complex.
