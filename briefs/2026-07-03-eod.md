## Market Recap

A badly missed June payrolls print (+57K actual vs. 110–115K expected; prior two months revised down a combined 74K) triggered the sharpest sector bifurcation of the year: the Dow Jones climbed 1.14% to a fresh all-time high of 52,900 while the Nasdaq-100 fell 1.61% — a 2.7-point divergence on a single session. Semiconductors bore the brunt: SOXX extended a two-day rout to total –9%+ from its high, with the SOX index losing 6.7% on July 1 alone after Bloomberg reported Meta is building a cloud business to resell its surplus AI computing capacity — crushing AMD specifically (its anchor hyperscaler customer) while lifting META itself. The weak jobs print knocked September rate-hike probability from ~70% to ~20% and caused the 2-year yield to fall 3.5bps to 4.13%, but the rate-relief bid went to Dow blue chips (AAPL, McDonald's, Disney), not the Nasdaq, which is the clearest signal yet that the AI-semi trade is unwinding on supply and margin concerns, not just macro.

## Tier 1: One Line Per Ticker

**AMD — ~$511.67 (–5.0%):** Two-day damage from the June 30 ATH of $580.91 now totals –11.9%; the Meta cloud-compute announcement (July 1) is the specific catalyst — Meta holds warrants for ~10% of AMD shares tied to a 6-gigawatt Instinct GPU deployment, and the signal that Meta has surplus compute to resell implies AMD's gigawatt 2–6 call-options are at risk; the stock is now approaching the psychologically important $500 level.

**AVGO — $369.34 (est. –2% to –4% on July 2):** Down approximately 24% since reporting June 3 earnings — the culprit was guidance: Q3 AI chip revenue guided to $16B vs. $17.2B consensus, full-year FY2027 AI revenue kept at "$100B+" rather than raised; the OpenAI "Jalapeño" custom inference ASIC partnership (announced June 24) is a genuine positive catalyst for 2027 but has not offset the guidance disappointment; next earnings September 3 is the next catalyst gate.

**SMCI — ~$25.41 (–8.1% from July 1's $27.65):** The legal crisis intensified materially on July 1 — Taiwan's Keelung District Prosecutors Office raided three affiliated SMCI Taiwan companies, detained two workers pending court hearing, and seized servers loaded with Nvidia chips being routed to mainland China, Hong Kong, and Macau through forged customs documents; Nvidia CEO reportedly urged SMCI to fix export compliance; stock is –39.8% YTD and has no fundamental floor until the legal overhang is resolved.

**MU — ~$975–988 (–4.3% on July 2; –10%+ on July 1):** Two-day total decline approaches 20% from the June 25 all-time high of $1,213.56; the selloff has three distinct drivers beyond macro: (1) a class-action lawsuit filed June 25 accusing MU, Samsung, and SK Hynix of colluding to restrict supply and inflate DRAM/NAND prices by up to 700% since 2022; (2) CEO Sanjay Mehrotra sold over $45M in stock near the highs; (3) the Meta/surplus-compute news spooks HBM demand assumptions; fundamental AI memory thesis intact but expectations were priced for perfection.

**MRVL — ~$245–272 (est. –10.3%):** Hardest-hit large-cap semi on the July 2 session; down 20–25% from its June 4 ATH of $316.43; two compounding headwinds beyond sector pressure: CFO Willem Meintjes liquidated nearly half his stock holdings (a significant insider signal), and MACD momentum just crossed below signal line; fundamental story (Q1 FY2027 revenue $2.418B +28% YoY; Q2 guided +35% YoY) is strong, but the stock ran +45% in a single month before the unwind.

**META — ~$584–590 (–3.8%):** Gave back part of July 1's spectacular +8.8–9% gain (which was driven by the Bloomberg scoop that Meta is building a cloud business to monetize its surplus AI compute — market read this as a structural monetization of the $710B capex cycle); July 2 pullback reflects tech sector cross-pressure and the dual-edged nature of the announcement (META surplus compute = less AMD demand urgency); META remains the cleanest fundamental story in the group with positive AI ROI narrative vs. peers.

**VST — ~$150.86 (–2.15%):** Modest pullback despite utilities broadly outperforming on the defensive bid; VST's identity as an AI data center power play (not a pure defensive utility) means it didn't get the full staples/utilities flight-to-safety lift; Nvidia-backed infrastructure vehicle has identified VST as a preferred supplier; median analyst target $232; Q2 earnings expected around August 5.

**UBER — $72.66 (% change unconfirmed; traded $71.67–$73.80):** No stock-specific catalyst on July 2; moved with the macro backdrop; Q1 2026 was strong (20% trip growth, 25% gross bookings growth, EPS $0.72 +33%); Q2 guided for 18–22% gross bookings growth; next earnings August 4–5.

**CCJ — ~$95–101 (est. –5% to –10% from recent highs):** Pulled back sharply from the June 30 high of ~$107.95 and the June 23 close of $108.89; caught in the broader energy/AI-adjacent risk-off; uranium spot ~$85/lb steady; no negative fundamental news — Cameco holds 230M lbs of long-term uranium contracts (~28M lbs/year through 2030), owns 49% of Westinghouse (AP1000 reactor OEM), and US DOE has committed up to $17.5B in conditional nuclear loan commitments; analyst consensus Buy, median target $134.20.

## Rotation Tracker

**Two-day verdict: the great AI-semi unwind has begun.** SOXX is down ~9% over July 1–2 (from $640+ to $584) while the Dow posted an all-time high — the two markets are moving in opposite directions in real time. Within tech, AAPL +4.8% vs. Nasdaq -1.61% on the same session is the sharpest intra-tech divergence of the year; investors are not leaving tech broadly, they're exiting the AI-capex-speculation tier and rotating into quality/consumer names. The BofA Bubble Risk Indicator for semis hit 0.91 out of 1.0 (near-maximum bubble reading) with SOXX having roughly doubled in H1 2026 and trading 60% above its 200-day moving average — Michael Burry has also issued a public semi bubble warning this year. The July 2 weak jobs print accelerated the rotation by removing the "strong economy supports AI capex" argument; if June's payroll miss is a trend, hyperscaler capex discipline conversations intensify. Money went: OUT of semis (SOXX –5.6% July 2) → INTO Dow defensives/blue chips. No signs of a stabilizing bid yet in the semi group.
