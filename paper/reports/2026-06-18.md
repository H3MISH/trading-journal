## Lessons Learned

1. **The diversifier carried the book — concentration in one theme nearly sank it.** Two of three positions (AMD, MRVL) were semiconductors, so when SOXX fell −5.92% on 06-16 the book had no offset *except* VST. VST (a non-semi AI-power name) was the single best trade and the only thing that turned a losing realized tape into ~breakeven. **Takeaway: deliberately uncorrelated exposure within a theme is worth more than a third correlated bet.** The Day-3 decision to keep the 4th slot empty rather than add another chip name was vindicated.

2. **Stops protect, but a tight trailing stop in a choppy name books you out of the rebound.** AMD's raised $520 stop fired on the 06-17 intraday plunge to ~$507 — then the stock rebounded +4.5% the same day on the Rackspace deal. The stop did its job (capped a small win, avoided the plunge), but a stop set inside normal daily volatility on a high-beta name will get hit on noise. **Takeaway: size the stop to the instrument's volatility, not to a round profit number.**

3. **Honoring the thesis-broken rule beat waiting for the stop.** MRVL was cut at −4.2% on the range break + CFO/insider overhang, *above* its $276 stop, because its only catalyst (06-22 index inclusion) fell outside the window. Selling on the broken thesis rather than waiting for the mechanical stop avoided additional downside. **Takeaway: a catalyst that lands after your horizon is not a reason to hold.**

4. **Under-deployment cost the benchmark race.** The book carried large cash (~74% on Day 4) and never held its 4-position max. That conservatism contained losses but also guaranteed underperformance in an up-market week. **Takeaway: with only 5 sessions, capital not deployed early can't compound — small position count + late-week "no trade" defaults means the upside ceiling is low by construction.**

5. **Process > a single week's number.** Two wins, one loss, every entry with a written thesis/stop/target, every exit rule-driven, zero rule violations — but a +0.02% result that trailed SPY. **Takeaway: a five-day window is mostly noise; the right scorecard is whether the rules were followed and risk was controlled, both of which held. Over more sessions the under-deployment and tight-stop habits are the things to fix, not the discipline.**

## Meta-Lesson: the state-management hazard

The single biggest operational risk this run was **not** market risk — it was **state continuity**. No `main` branch ever existed, so each scheduled run landed on a fresh harness-assigned branch and the naive behavior would have been to re-initialize a brand-new $1,000 Day-1 portfolio every time (as the abandoned 06-10 and 06-11 runs did). Locating the true latest state across 20 branches and continuing it — rather than resetting — is what made this a coherent 5-day simulation instead of five disconnected Day-1s. **Takeaway: when persistence is fragile, verifying and threading prior state forward is a prerequisite to the task, not an afterthought.**
